# Dollars and shillings are two ledgers; receipts keep their rate, totals use today's

The college takes and spends Somaliland shillings as well as US dollars, so every payment and expense carries its currency, and the two are never added together when they're stored: every total is kept per currency, the way a college with two cash drawers keeps two books. Converting everything into dollars as it came in was rejected, because a receipt has to say what the student actually handed over and a drawer of shillings has to be counted in shillings. Keeping the two apart with no way to see them together was rejected too, because the admin needs one figure for how a branch is doing.

A payment or expense is a receipt, and a receipt doesn't change. A shilling one keeps the exchange rate in force when it was recorded, and what it was worth in dollars that day, worked out once to the cent and stored on the row. The admin sets the rate on the Settings page, and every change is kept.

Totals are a different question: what the college's money is worth now. So every combined figure, on every screen, is the dollars plus the shillings at today's rate, and it moves when the rate does; if a dollar was 10,000 shillings last month and is 12,000 now, last month's shillings count for less. The first version added up each row's own dollar value instead, so a past month never moved. It was dropped because the admin reads the dashboard as what the college has, and a figure built from old rates no longer says that. As a result the ≈ dollar value shown under a shilling amount, which is at its own day's rate, doesn't add up to the totals, and each screen says so. Every screen uses the same rate, so they all agree on the same total.

Fees stay priced in dollars, and what a student owes stays in dollars; paying in shillings is paying that fee at today's rate. A percentage teacher earns in whatever currency the student paid, and each currency's share is paid out of its own ledger. A fixed salary is set in one currency and paid in it. The monthly budget is planned in dollars and compared against the combined figures. The database itself checks that a dollar row has no rate and is worth its amount, and that a shilling row has a rate and a dollar value that matches it.
